Huddle Up by Lesson Shuffle

A team review game where nobody can win alone.

Paste your terms, pick a way to play, and students join on their own devices with a room code. Every teammate holds a few of the answers, so the only way to win is to talk to each other. There are no student accounts and no ads, and it runs in the browser, with nothing to install.

Why I made it

Years ago I started playing team matching games with my students, and I could not stop changing the rules. One team would race ahead while another never got to finish, so we started playing to eleven. Then we tried it loud, then silent, then as a relay around the room. Those games had the whole class talking about vocabulary.

Not every school can use the big quiz sites, though, often because of student data privacy rules. So I built a simple version that keeps nothing about students, with all of those ways to play built in.

Get started

  1. Choose Start a game on your computer, ideally the one connected to your projector.
  2. Paste your terms, one pair on each line, or press Try a sample list. A tab, a comma, a colon or a dash between the term and its match all work. You need at least 4 pairs, and 12 or more works best.
  3. Pick a way to play and press Open a room. The board shows a six-character room code and a QR code.
  4. Students go to apps.lessonshuffle.com/join (or scan the QR code), type the code and a nickname, and press Join.
  5. Press Make teams. Each student's screen shows their team and teammates. Press Start the round and the countdown begins.

Ways to play

Every way to play uses the same screens. What changes is how students sit, move and talk. The rule for each one shows on the board and on every student's screen.

Team Race

Teams sit together. Each round every teammate sees the same question, but only one of them holds the right answer. First team to the finish wins.

Eleven

In a race, the team that falls behind never gets to finish. In Eleven, every team keeps playing until it reaches eleven, so everyone works through the whole list. The board times each team and remembers its best time, so you can challenge teams to beat it next round. Shuffle the teams often so no one feels stuck on the slow team.

Cacophony

Students stay in their seats, wherever they are, and call out across the room to find their teammates. It gets loud, and students quickly learn that the louder they are, the less they can hear each other. Not for every room or every day, but the excitement is worth it.

Silent

The same game with no talking at all. Teams use hand signals: a thumbs up, the first letter of an answer, hands up when unsure. The first time, press Start 2 minutes of planning time on the teams screen so teams can agree on their signals. It is one of the harder ways to play, so mix it in with the others.

Relay

Set each team's devices side by side and have students line up behind them, so they cannot see the screens. One runner at a time checks every screen, makes the match, then tags the next runner. After each match the team's screens say Next runner! Everyone is accountable and everyone moves, so you need some room. You decide whether teammates in line may call out help.

Tag Team

A silent game. Half of each team sits with heads down in front of two devices, and the other half stands behind them. Standers read the screens and tap a shoulder, left or right, to say which device has the answer. The sitter looks up and answers on that device. Two students touch every answer, and no one can carry the whole team.

Playoffs

String several games together. Each game's winning team moves on and sits out the rest of that round. When half the teams have moved on, the field is cut in half again, until two teams meet in the final. Teams that are waiting or knocked out are not left out: they play a consolation game at the same time, shown below the playoff game on the board. Before each playoff game you can pick its style, such as Team Race, Silent or Relay.

Using it

Students write the set

In the room, press Students write the set. Students see a form on their screens and can send in a term and what it means, up to 10 each. Each one shows on your screen with its sender's nickname. Press Keep to add it to the list, or Drop to leave it out. Writing the questions is great review on its own.

Settings

Choose the matches to win (6 to 20), the number of teams (or about 3 per team), whether students see the meaning and find the term, the other way round, or a mix, and what a wrong answer costs: a point, everything (back to zero) or nothing. A student who taps a wrong answer waits 3 seconds, and the whole team sees the right match for a moment.

Names

Students type a nickname or first name, or press Pick one for me. You can also give everyone a random name, like Swift Comet. In the room, New name swaps any name for a random one, and the X removes a player.

Teams

Teams are named for places around a school, like the Media Center or the Makerspace, and each has its own colour so students can find each other fast. Random student names pair a describing word with a classroom object, like Brass Compass. Teams are shuffled every round unless you turn that off. A student who joins late goes onto the smallest team.

After each round

The board shows how every team did and the matches teams missed most, with no names attached. That list is a quick place to start reteaching.

Saving your lists

Press Save this list in this browser to keep a term list for next time. Saved lists stay on your computer only. You can also import a .txt or .csv file, so a list from a spreadsheet or a flashcard tool's export works too.

To give a list to a colleague, press Copy a share link. The words ride inside the link itself, so nothing is uploaded. Pictures stay with you.

Pictures and sound

Press Add pictures to put a picture with any match. Students see it with the meaning, which helps with diagrams, maps, artwork and younger readers. A tap makes it bigger. Pictures are shrunk on your computer and only travel to students' screens during the game.

The board plays a short sound for each match and a short tune at the finish. Press Sound on to turn it off. Students' devices stay quiet.

When a team misses a match, that one comes back a few questions later as a second chance, so the tough ones get another look.

Something not working?

  • A student sees “We could not find that room”? Check the code on the board. Codes never use the letters I or O, or the numbers 0 or 1.
  • A student's screen says “Waiting for your teacher's screen”? Your computer lost its connection for a moment. It reconnects by itself, and so do students.
  • A student refreshed or their device went to sleep? They can open the join page again in the same tab, and they keep their name and team.
  • You refreshed your own page? The game picks up where it left off in that tab. Closing the tab ends it.
  • No answers on a student's screen? On a big team there can be more teammates than answers. That student helps the team find it.
  • Nothing connects at all? Your school's network may block the game's connection. Ask your technology team to allow rooms.lessonshuffle.com.

Frequently asked questions

Do students need an account?

No. Students only type the room code and a nickname. There is no sign-in for you or for them.

Is it free? Are there ads?

It is free, and there are no ads.

How many students can play?

Up to 80 devices in a room, in up to 10 teams.

What devices work?

Any device with a current web browser: Chromebooks, laptops, tablets and phones. Your own screen works best on a computer connected to the projector.

Can I use pictures?

Yes. Press Add pictures under your term list and pick a picture for any match. Students see it with the meaning and find the term that goes with it.

Can my district use it?

Huddle Up was built for schools with strict student data rules, like New York's Education Law 2-d. It asks for no accounts and no email addresses, and it saves nothing about students. Every district decides for itself, so please share the privacy policy with your data privacy officer.

Your privacy in plain words

  • The game runs in your browser tab. Nicknames, teams and scores live there, and they are gone when you close the room or the tab.
  • Students' devices reach your tab through a small relay at rooms.lessonshuffle.com. It only passes messages along. It does not save names, answers or scores, and it keeps no logs of them.
  • No student accounts, no email addresses, no cookies and no ads. Students only type a room code and a nickname.
  • The student join page has no page counter at all. The teacher pages count visits anonymously, like the rest of this site.
  • Term lists and pictures you save stay in your own browser, on your computer. A share link holds the words inside the link, not on a server.
